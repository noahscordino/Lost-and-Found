The problem, close to home

It's not just a global issue.

Textile waste is one of New Zealand's fastest-growing waste streams. Cheap, fast-turnaround clothing — a lot of it now arriving directly from overseas retailers — gets worn a handful of times and thrown out, and most of it has nowhere to go but landfill.

Once in the ground, synthetic fibres don't break down cleanly. They shed microplastics and release methane as they decompose — a big part of why the fashion industry's climate footprint in NZ is larger than it looks from the outside.

And it's a growing habit, not a shrinking one. The rise of ultra-cheap overseas retailers has made it easier than ever to buy clothes that were never built to last — which means more of it ends up as waste, faster.

Zooming out

The global picture isn't much better.

The fashion industry produces an estimated 92 million tonnes of textile waste globally every year — and that figure is projected to keep climbing as fast fashion accelerates. Less than 1% of clothing material is ever recycled back into new garments.

The Ellen MacArthur Foundation puts it starkly: every second, the equivalent of a garbage truck full of clothes is burned or buried somewhere in the world. Fashion now accounts for roughly 8–10% of global carbon emissions — more than international aviation and shipping combined.

The uncomfortable part

Donating isn't always the fix people think it is.

It's easy to assume a bag dropped at a charity bin is problem solved. In reality, op shops are flooded with far more donated clothing than they can ever sell — a lot of it low-value fast fashion — and a significant share of what doesn't sell is quietly landfilled anyway.

That's not a knock on op shops, who do genuinely good work with what they can move. It's the reason we built Lost & Found around resale first, donation second — rather than treating donation as the default outcome for everything we take in.

Our part in fixing it.

We're a small East Auckland business, not a global fix — but here's how Lost & Found is actually built to keep clothing out of the ground.

🔁
Resale-first, always
Quality clothing goes through The Arkhives and gets genuinely worn again — not just diverted to a bin that may or may not move it on.
🎯
Selective by design
We're picky about what we collect for free pickup so every trip is worthwhile — less driving for low-value loads, more capacity for collections that matter.

Real donation partners
